---
name: ykwim
description: Confirm understanding before doing any work. Use when the user's message ends with "ykwim?", "you know what I mean?", "know what I mean?", "kwim?", "if you catch my drift", or a similar check that you've understood what they're asking.
---

The user is asking whether you actually understood the request. Answer that question before doing anything else.

1. Do no work yet. No edits, no commands that change state, no delegating. Read-only lookups are fine if you need a fact to resolve an ambiguity: look it up yourself rather than asking.
2. Restate the request in one or two sentences, in your own words: what you will change or answer, and where.
3. Name anything ambiguous or assumed: unclear referents ("it", "that file"), scope, which of several readings you'd pick, constraints you're guessing at. If there are none, say so plainly.
4. For each real ambiguity, ask one short question and give your recommended answer, so the user can reply "yes" or "1 yes, 2 no, do X".
5. Stop and wait for the user's reply. Start the work only after they confirm or correct you.

If the restatement is clear and nothing is ambiguous, still stop and wait for the go-ahead. The point of the trigger is that the user wants to see your understanding first.
